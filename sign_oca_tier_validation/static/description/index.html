Sign Oca Tier Validation

This module lets the reviewers of a tier validation (base_tier_validation) sign their tiers in a sign_oca document. It works with any model that inherits tier.validation.

Once a validation is requested, the record sends one sign request whose document is provided by the model:

  • each tier review is a signer, with the sign role of its tier definition (created on the fly the first time);
  • any reviewer of the tier may sign it; the first one who signs holds the role, and the document shows their name;
  • the tiers are signed in the order that base_tier_validation enforces (Approve by sequence): a reviewer gets the email inviting them to sign when their tier’s turn comes;
  • signing approves the tier, refusing (sign_oca_refuse) rejects it, exactly as the Validate and Reject buttons of the record do; the comment written in the document becomes the comment of the review; with sign_oca_pades, a refused document gets its certificate and final seal as a completed one does, and the seal of a signature names the reviewer as the document does;
  • while the document is out for signature, no tier is decided outside it: the buttons of the record, the comment wizard and any call made as the user are refused; the request is cancelled with its validation only, not by a user, its responsible included;
  • the request always requires a login (sign_oca_login): the reviewer signs as their own user;
  • the name, date and status fields of a tier are written by the server, so the signed document states who decided, when, and what;
  • the signing page shows the steps of the validation next to the document, with each tier’s status, the time of its decision and its comment.

Configuration

  • On each tier definition (Settings / Technical / Tier Validations / Tier Definition), the Sign Role field gives the role its reviewers sign as. It is created with the tier’s name when a first document is sent; it can be set beforehand to reuse an existing role.
  • A tier whose definition has Comment checked requires the comment field of the document to be filled in to sign.

Usage

Sign a validation

  • Request the validation of the record as usual.
  • Send it for signature: the model calls record._sign_oca_create_request() or offers the action_sign_oca_create_request button. The Signatures smart button of the record opens its sign requests.
  • The reviewers of the first tier get an email with the link of the document. They log in, fill in their signature (and their comment), and validate; or press Refuse and give a reason.
  • The reviewers of the next tier get their email once the previous tier is approved.
  • Restarting the validation cancels the open request. While the request is open, the Validate and Reject buttons of the record refuse to decide: the tiers are decided in the document.
  • A tier whose definition has Approve Sequence Bypass is approved along with an earlier tier signed by one of its reviewers, as base_tier_validation does from the record. It needs no signature: the document gets the reviewer’s name, the date and the status Approved (same reviewer), its signature and comment stay empty, and the request completes without it.
  • Once signed, the page of the signer links to the document, to download or view.

Fields of a tier

Each field of a tier’s role has a kind, read from the tier_item key of the item in signatory_data or, without it, from its sign field:

Kind Sign field Value
signature Signature drawn by the reviewer; always required
name Name Name (login) of the user who decides, written by the server
date Date date and time of the decision, YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM, written by the server
status Status Approved or Refused, written by the server
comment Comment written by the reviewer, on several lines (Multi-line Text field); required when the tier asks for a comment; the reason on a refusal

The date and status are Multi-line Text fields too, so that their text starts at the top of their box, as the comment does. The date is in the time zone kept on the request when it is created: the one of the record’s company, else of the user who sends it, else UTC (tier.validation._sign_oca_tier_get_tz()). The status is in the language of the record’s company, else of the user. The values sent by the browser for the name, date and status are ignored, and the signing page does not show these fields to fill.

With sign_oca_anchor installed, the fields are placed from the anchors of the document. A tier’s role is named tier<n>, n being the tier number of the review (its Tier, 1 for the first tier to decide), and the field by its kind:

[[sig:tier1:signature]] [[sig:tier1:name]] [[sig:tier1:date]]
[[sig:tier1:status]] [[sig:tier1:comment]]

For developers

  • tier.validation._sign_oca_get_document() returns (pdf_bytes, filename), the document to sign. Each model implements it.
  • tier.validation._sign_oca_prepare_items(sign_request) places the fields of the draft request; override it to place them by code, with a tier_item key on each item. A document that embeds files written by others calls sign_request._sign_oca_tier_place_items(pages=...) with the pages it prints itself, so that text typed in those files places no field.
  • tier.validation._sign_oca_tier_get_tz() returns the time zone of the dates of the document.
  • tier.validation._sign_oca_tier_decided_in_document() tells whether the tiers of a record are decided in a document only (default: while a tier request is open); _sign_oca_tier_check_backoffice_decision() refuses then any decision taken as the user, which validate_tier, reject_tier, _validate_tier and _rejected_tier call. The signing page decides as superuser.
  • sign_oca_anchor’s anchors take a k option: _sign_oca_tier_place_items(key=...) places only the anchors carrying that key, so that a document printing text written by its users draws a key when it is rendered and ignores the anchors typed in it.
  • tier.validation._sign_oca_create_request(message="") creates and sends the request of the open reviews, or returns the open one.
  • sign.oca.request.signer._sign_oca_tier_item_values(user, decision, moment) returns the values the server writes; _sign_oca_tier_stamped_name(user) the name it writes.
  • sign.oca.request._sign_oca_tier_done_signers() returns the signers whose tier is done: signed, or approved by bypass (tier_bypassed). sign.oca.request.signer._sign_oca_tier_bypass_reviews(user) returns the reviews an approval by user approves too.
  • A decision stamped without a signature (a refusal, a tier approved by bypass) passes its status and stamped name in the context key sign_oca_stamp_reason; with sign_oca_pades, the seal of that stamp records it as its reason.
  • The steps panel is the QWeb template sign_oca_tier_validation.tier_steps_panel. The empty templates tier_steps_intro (above the steps) and tier_steps_start (first item of the list of steps, for a step 0) are hooks for other modules. sign.oca.request.signer._sign_oca_tier_status_labels() gives the labels of the steps, and _sign_oca_tier_item_placeholders() the placeholders of the empty fields of the signing page, in the language of the page.
  • The link between a request and a validation (is_tier_validation, tier_review_ids, the signers’ tier_review_id) is written as superuser only, and a signer signs only the open request of the record its review validates.
  • Every reviewer of a tier reads its request and signer, and finds it in the portal list of their sign requests and in the systray counter.
  • A model sets _sign_oca_tier_smart_button = False to keep its form view without the Signatures smart button.

Known issues / Roadmap

  • A tier decided by code running as superuser outside the document leaves its signer unsigned: the request stays open. A rejection cancels it; an approval does not.
